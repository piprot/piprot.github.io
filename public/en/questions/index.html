FAQ

Most people come to me stuck in one of these places

People who ask tend to circle a few directions. These are grouped into five categories below so you can find where you are stuck. How to actually break it down depends on your specific situation — the categories are for navigation, not diagnosis, and your case may not fit just one.

01Resume & interview prep 02Job direction & career change 03Internships & experience building 04Offers & career growth 05Mindset & pace of action

01 · Direction

Resume & interview prep

The most typical opening is: I have rewritten my resume many times and still get no replies. This looks like a writing problem but is usually a fit problem.

I have rewritten my resume many times with no replies — is the wording not good enough?
If an experience does not fill three lines, does that mean it is not worth writing?
When an interviewer asks why I left, will telling the truth cost me points?
The interview felt good but there was no follow-up — how do I review it usefully?

02 · Choice

Job direction & career change

Can I switch, where to, and should I move now are three phrasings of one question, and all must pass through resource inventory first.

I can cope with my current job but see no direction — should I move?
I want to change careers but lack relevant experience — where do I start?
Is switching in my thirties too late?
Two industries both look good — how do I compare them?

03 · Building experience

Internships & experience building

When experience feels thin, the first reaction is to add another stint. More often the three you already have were never explained well.

My experience looks too thin — do I have to add another internship?
How do I handle a gap on my resume — exam prep, family matters, or unemployment?
My work was fragmented and reads like a log — what now?
I did few school projects — can I include coursework?

04 · Decisions

Offers & career growth

These suit a structured checklist: list benefits and costs separately, then test them from a three-year-out perspective.

Two offers — one pays more, one has a better platform. How do I choose?
Should I name a number first in salary talks?
A small company with a senior title versus a big company with a junior one — how to weigh them?
I accepted an offer and regret it — can I back out?

05 · Pace

Mindset & pace of action

What needs adjusting is not your mood but the size and order of your actions. Making step one small enough beats repeated pep talks.

Lots of applications, no feedback, and it is getting messier — how do I stop?
I know what to do but cannot move — is this a discipline problem?
My family sees it differently — should I listen?
Always preparing, never daring to apply — what now?

How I usually break it down

Three actions, in order

The five categories look different, but in action they follow basically one order. Reverse it and you usually go in circles.

Step 1

Inventory resources

Go through nine dimensions first — time, finances, knowledge, work, psychology, relationships, body, life, and spirit. Ask what I have before what I want to do.

Step 2

Set the frame

List benefits and costs clearly, assign priorities, then test from a long-term view to avoid agonizing over one point.

Step 3

Line up actions

Land the conclusion in three small things you can do next week, run one round, then review. Do not plan everything at once or turn the plan into a wish list.
