Digitalization isn't buying software — first map who does what at each step

2026-10-30 · Yunyue Junling

Many companies take choosing software as the first step toward digitalization. The system arrives, but the process is still messy and there are still two sets of data. The problem is not the tool but the order. What digitalization must really solve is where information breaks and where responsibility blurs — and that requires mapping the process before picking software.

Step 1: Map the process, not the org chart

Follow one customer or one order all the way from intake to delivery, marking each step: who does it, what it produces, and who receives it. An org chart tells you who reports to whom; a process map tells you how work actually flows.

Breakpoints usually appear in three places: handoffs with no record, approvals by word of mouth, and key data held by one individual.

Step 2: Define data, not features

At each step, answer two questions first: what it needs tosee, and what it needs tokeep. Define the fields and their definitions before discussing features.

If definitions are not aligned, two sets of numbers will fight each other once the system goes live. Aligning definitions then costs far more than doing it at the start.

Step 3: Choose tools that follow the process

Tools amplify a process; they do not replace it. If the process is unclear, any system just converts manual inefficiency into digital inefficiency.

Only with process and data in place do tools have somewhere to land; otherwise you have bought a more expensive mess.

Three signals that it is time for a system

  • The same data must be entered in three places.
  • Handoffs rely on chat and word of mouth, leaving no trace.
  • A single report takes half a day to assemble by hand and often does not reconcile.

Digitalization is not a one-shot project: get one main thread working first, then connect the rest one by one. Map that thread clearly and the money goes where it matters.
