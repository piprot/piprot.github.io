The Wheel of Life isn't a scoring game — it shows where your time actually goes

2026-10-20 · Yunyue Junling

Many people do a monthly review by scoring all eight dimensions, then set it aside. The scores themselves mean nothing; what matters is whether they match your actual investment. The real use of the Wheel of Life is to compare it with your time log and find the segment you claim to value but never invest in.

Scores are feelings, time is evidence

You scored health an 8, but how many times did you actually exercise this month? When feeling and evidence disagree, that segment is self-deception. The first step of a review is not scoring but seeing clearly where your time went.

Find the segments you claim to value but do not invest in

Usually there are one or two: important in words, near zero on the time log. That segment is often the only one you need to change next month.

Change only one segment a month

Changing all eight is the same as changing none. Pick one and pair it with a verifiable action — for example, twice a week for thirty minutes — and put it in the calendar, not on a wish list.

A review is not about scoring yourself; it is about making what matters actually appear on your calendar.

A ten-minute practice

Open this month's calendar, sort your time into the eight dimensions, then revisit your scores. Put the segment with the biggest gap on the first line of next month's plan. One segment is enough.

Managing time is really managing who you give your attention to. See it clearly first, then decide.
