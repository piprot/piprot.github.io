Reading a hundred books a year is less useful than breaking one problem into four layers

2026-10-23 · Yunyue Junling

Reading a lot does not mean thinking clearly. The real difference is knowing how to break things down. In the same situation, some see only the symptom while others see the structure. Split a problem into symptom, cause, structure, and action, and what you read becomes your own judgment rather than someone else's conclusion.

Layer 1: Symptom — what actually happened

Separate facts from interpretations first. Most arguments happen because two people have not even agreed on what happened, yet are already arguing about what to do.

Layer 2: Cause — why it happened

Distinguish direct causes from root causes. Ask one more layer of 'and then?', and you will often see where the real lever is.

Layer 3: Structure — what system it happens in

Look at incentives, processes, roles, and resources. The same behavior may not happen at all under a different structure. Many people problems are actually structure problems.

Layer 4: Action — which part I can change

Land on what to do next week. Thinking without action is only more refined anxiety.

Breaking things down isn't about thinking harder — it's about knowing which layer to act on.

A ten-minute exercise

Take the problem that has been bothering you most lately and write one sentence for each of the four layers. You will likely find that there are only one or two places you can actually act, and they are not on the layer you first assumed.

Books are other people's answers; breaking down is how you make the question your own.
