Rewrote your resume ten times with no reply? The problem is rarely the wording

2026-11-06 · Yunyue Junling

The most time-consuming part of editing a resume is often the wording: stronger verbs, more adjectives. But recruiters spend only ten-odd seconds on a resume, scanning first for whether you fit the role, not for literary flair. Few replies usually come down to three structural problems, not unpretty sentences.

1. First answer: which role are you applying for

Sending one resume to every role is the most common way to lose points. The other side judges fit within ten seconds, and a generic resume gives no signal.

The method is simple: before applying, write one sentence on the core problem this role solves, then move the two most relevant experiences to the top. Adjusting the first third of the same draft for different roles is far more effective than rewriting the whole thing.

2. Replace what I did with what I achieved

Anyone can write a job description; results are the evidence. Write each experience in three sentences: what the situation was, what you specifically did, and what changed.

Numbers need not be exaggerated but need a reference — from what to what, over what period, covering how many people. Where there are no numbers, at least write what was different because of your involvement.

3. Priorities: put the strongest evidence first

Recruiters read a resume as an inverted pyramid: the first third decides whether they keep reading. The most relevant experience, the hardest results, and the skills matching the role must appear within that first screen.

A resume is not about writing everything about yourself; it is about getting the other person to decide within ten seconds that they want to talk.

A pre-submission checklist

  • Can you explain the core problem of the target role in one sentence?
  • Does each experience show results, or only responsibilities?
  • Does the first third contain the most relevant content?
  • Was this version tailored specifically to this role?

In the end, resumes are not compared on whose words are fancier but on who helps the reader complete the fit judgment sooner. Lower the cost of that judgment and replies will follow.
