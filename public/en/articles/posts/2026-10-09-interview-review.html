From “I assumed” to “I verified”: a interviewee interview retrospective

2026-10-09 · Yunyue Junling

The easiest mistake in proposals is treating “what I think the interviewee wants” as the need. In one interview, the interviewee said they wanted A; on probing, the real blocker turned out to be B. In reviewing that interview, I broke it into three steps: how to ask, how to record, and how to verify.

What interviewees say and what they need are often different things

A interviewee asked for “faster reports.” Asking “who reads them faster, and what they do next” revealed the real problem: decisions were too slow. The report was just the first solution that came to mind, not necessarily the best one.

How to ask: keep asking “and then?” and “why now?”

Replace “what do you want?” with “how are you doing it now, and where are you stuck?” The first gets you wishes; the second gets you the reality on the ground.

How to record: keep verbatim words separate from your judgment

Record their exact words in one column and your judgments in another. Looking back, you'll know which statements are facts and which are your own interpretations. Mix the two and the retrospective becomes self-confirmation.

How to verify: test with the smallest possible action

Showing a minimal version beats ten rounds of debate in a meeting. Only when the other person points and says “yes, that's it” is the need actually confirmed.

Needs aren't asked into existence — you close in on them through probing and verification.

Three things to do after an interview

  • Write down three “I assumed” statements and find evidence for each.
  • Highlight judgments with no evidence in red and ask about them specifically next time.
  • Use a minimal version to verify the most critical one.

Only when “I assumed” becomes “I verified” does a proposal stand up.
