01 / How to begin

Diagnose first, then discuss solutions

Proposing solutions before seeing the situation clearly easily puts effort on the wrong layer. So before formal engagement there is always a session to clarify the problem.

1

30-minute free diagnostic call

Both sides align on the boundaries of the need and achievable goals: what you truly want to change, what known solutions can cover, and what requires people to move first. If both judge it a poor fit after talking, we stop there.

2

Write down your situation before consulting

Fill in an intake questionnaire in advance: current role, team state, key people, top worries, and what you have tried. The more specific you are, the less time diagnosis takes. Anything awkward to write can be said in the conversation.

3

Tailored plan and written confirmation

After confirming scope, we produce a Goals and Milestones Plan and a written proposal, and sign an NDA and service contract. Duration and fees are set by project goals, complexity, and time window, made clear at this step rather than fixed on the page.

4

Advance by milestones

Each session centers on one axis; within 24 hours afterwards you receive session notes and action commitments. Responses to daily questions and urgent matters follow what the plan sets out.

5

Mid-term review and closing

We assess progress midway and adjust goals or pace if needed. At closing you receive a Results Retrospective Report and follow-up suggestions, turning the judgment formed in this period into something reusable.

02 / What we do together

Break what should I do into actionable steps

Private coaching takes the one thing in front of you and breaks it open with you, rather than lecturing through a methodology.

Layer the problem

Separate the part that known solutions can cover from the part requiring people to change positions, habits, or relationships. Cut it wrong and every later action just adds force on the same layer.

Key people and interest map

Lay out management, key staff, predecessors, superiors, and collaborators: what each wants, fears, and can influence. Draw the map first, then discuss the order of conversations.

Decision process and risk contingencies

See how information flows and who should know at each step, turn key actions into a checklist with acceptance criteria, and prepare a fallback for anything that could go out of control.

Three review questions and transfer

What happened and on what basis I judged; which principle that judgment matches; whether I would choose the same in another setting. Add one more: am I responding to the problem or to my own unease?

Most of the time, three rounds of questions save more time than presenting a plan first. Once I give the answer too early, your real situation gets covered by my framework.

04 / Common situations

Three kinds of people who come to me

Different starting points, not different ability: the resources you hold, the risks you carry, and the first thing you need to practice well all differ.

01 / Landing period

Find your footing in an unfamiliar organization

Common situation:The predecessor is still in the team, old habits clash with new demands, and superiors' expectations are unclear.

Break it down:Handover strategy, mapping key people, the first team meeting, and how to frame your first-month report.

Watch out for:Copying wholesale what worked at your last company.

02 / Founders

Hold direction steady amid chaos

Common situation:Tight resources, messy information, shifting direction, and nobody to define the problem for you.

Break it down:Separate changing direction from changing tactics, and set verification points for key assumptions.

Watch out for:Redoing the whole strategy every time you hit resistance.

03 / High-potential talent

Moving things forward without positional authority

Common situation:You need cross-department results but have no authority to direct, so you rely on consensus and credibility.

Break it down:Shared gains, the other side's costs, and a verifiable first step.

Watch out for:Waiting until you have authority before practicing influence.

05 / Boundaries

Confidentiality, responsibility, and what is not promised

Four agreements

· Sign an NDA: questionnaire and session content are used only for this engagement and are not passed to third parties.
· I do not decide for you: I deliver diagnosis, structure, written notes, and verifiable actions; the final choice remains yours.
· No outcome or quick-fix promises: judgment grows through repeated moves and reviews, not one long conversation.
· No inflated results, and no using other people's cases to endorse a method.
